WP1

WP1 · 1 Oct 2026 – 31 Jan 2027

Design & development

WP1 establishes the conceptual and technical foundations of the benchmark and develops a prototype human-AI collaboration platform. We will adopt a participatory co-design approach, ensuring tasks and AI collaborator roles reflect realistic collaboration scenarios. We will conduct two workshops: an in-person UK workshop (~20 participants) to co-design task formats and evaluation criteria, and a hybrid workshop (~30 participants) via the Asia-Europe Foundation/UNESCO network to examine inclusivity and cross-cultural applicability. We will finalise task prompts, scoring rubrics, and analysis plan, and develop a dialogic coding framework by adapting Tech-SEDA (Hennessy, 2023) to human-AI collaboration.

In parallel, AI collaborators will be implemented as a multi-agent system, where each agent represents a specific collaborative role. These agents will be powered by large language models (LLMs) and configured through role-specific prompts, behavioural policies, and interaction constraints. We will employ state-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., OpenAI’s ChatGPT and Anthropic’s Claude models), given their reliability and strong performance in conversational reasoning. We will also experiment with open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA- or Qwen-based models) to assess portability and reproducibility across model ecosystems.

Following a review of existing platforms (e.g., Deliberate Lab, NarraNexus), we found that while they provide a foundation for studying collaborative behaviour, they do not support key experimental requirements, particularly concerning multi-agent, role-based interaction, systematic manipulation of AI transparency (e.g., uncertainty signals), and feedback-driven adaptation. To ensure feasibility within the project timeline, we will build on an existing platform (e.g., Deliberate Lab) and extend it to support the integration of AI collaborators with role-specific prompts, behavioural policies, and controlled manipulation of transparency conditions.

We will incorporate a lightweight human feedback mechanism, allowing participants to rate AI contributions (e.g., helpfulness, fairness, clarity). These signals will be used to iteratively refine prompts and interaction policies using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)-inspired approaches (Bai et al., 2022; Ouyang et al., 2022). Parameter-efficient methods such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (Hu et al., 2022) will be used to fine-tune both the reward model and the reinforcement learning policy. LoRA restricts training to a small set of adapter parameters, significantly reducing computational cost while maintaining strong performance. A pilot study will be carried out to validate the platform and finalise the experiment design.

WP2

WP2 · 1 Feb 2027 – 31 Jul 2027

Data collection & analysis

WP2 conducts the empirical evaluation using the WP1 benchmark and platform. Initial recruitment targets are ~315 participants per task, with final sample sizes and team configurations determined following the WP1 pilot and feasibility assessment. Participant recruitment and study delivery will be supported by Prolific’s infrastructure, which provides access to large, diverse participant pools and supports efficient online study management. This enables large-scale recruitment and coordination of synchronous sessions, ensuring feasibility and high-quality data collection.

Evaluation focuses on three complementary dimensions: 1) perceived usefulness and usability; 2) team outcome quality; and 3) collaboration processes. Participants will evaluate the usefulness and usability of the platform using the Technology Acceptance Model (Davis et al., 1989, TAM). Outcome quality will be assessed through human expert rubric-based ratings and, where appropriate, automated evaluation methods (e.g., automated argumentative essay scoring; Chen et al., 2024). We will also analyse the collective intelligence “c factor” (Woolley et al., 2010) to capture team-level performance. Collaboration processes will be analysed using behavioural and perceptual measures, including participation equality (e.g., turn-taking and word distribution), conversational balance, and perceived inclusion. Trust and reliance will be measured through behavioural indicators (e.g., acceptance or override of AI suggestions) and surveys, with trust calibration defined as alignment between trust, reliance, and AI performance.

To understand underlying mechanisms, we will apply the dialogic coding scheme developed in WP1 to a subset of dialogues (Wegerif and Casebourne, 2025; Long et al., 2024), analysing collaborative reasoning and knowledge construction.

WP3

WP3 · 1 Jul 2027 – 30 Sep 2027

Report & dissemination

WP3 consolidates findings and disseminates outputs to academic, policy, and practitioner communities. We will produce a final technical report describing the benchmark, results, and design implications for inclusive human-AI collaboration. Research papers will be submitted to leading venues (e.g., Nature, PNAS, NeurIPS, ACL, AAAI, EMNLP). Key artefacts will be released, including the benchmark, dataset, platform, and code. We will also produce a practitioner playbook outlining evidence-based guidance on AI collaborator roles, transparency design, and team configurations.

We will organise a dissemination workshop in September 2027, engaging stakeholders, partners, policymakers, and users to discuss findings and implications. The project will also develop a roadmap for follow-on funding to extend the benchmark to broader domains and inter-organisational collective intelligence settings.
